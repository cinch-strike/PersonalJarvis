# V · E

**SIX HUNDRED YEARS WAITING**

*The Elena Hunt — run sheet. For Bianca and anyone helping run or test this.
Not for guests.*

Print it, keep it in a pocket. Everything Vlad says is below word for word, so
you can tell "he did not understand" from "that is what he is supposed to say".

⚠️ **Spoilers throughout.** Do not leave this lying on a table.

---

## The shape of it

| # | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anywhere | Guest reads the opening card, works out it means the skull |
| 2 | At Vlad | Asks what he waits for → he says he has **forgotten**, two words are written somewhere |
| 3 | Around the house | Finds two hidden riddles → **WINTER** and **ROSES** |
| 4 | At Vlad | Says **"winter roses"** → he remembers, tells Elena's story |
| 5 | The garden | Finds the jawless skull in the **spa pool** |
| 6 | At Vlad | Says they found a skull → he asks what is missing |
| 7 | At Vlad | Says **"the jaw"** → sent to the lounge table, asked their name |
| 8 | Anywhere | **Must still work out who the descendant is.** Brings skull **and** jaw to them and says **"winter roses"**. Only **Donnie** replies — with congratulations |

**Only step 8 wins it, and it has three parts:** the right person, both objects,
and the words. Objects without the words is not a win. Words without the objects
is not a win. Either of those handed to the wrong adult is not a win — and that
adult must not let on.

---

## The three cards

### Opening card — leads them to Vlad

> One here has waited longer than these walls,  
> who owns no crown, no castle and no halls.  
> He will not raise it first. He is too proud.  
> But he will answer — if you ask aloud.  
> Not what he was, and not what he became.  
> Ask what he waits for. He'll tell you a name.  

### Hidden riddle 1 — answer: **WINTER**

> I strip the trees and starve the light,  
> I bring the coat, I bring the night.  
> Frost is my breath, dark comes at four —  
> name the season I stand for.  
> Speak me first, before you're through —  
> the second word depends on you.  

### Hidden riddle 2 — answer: **ROSES**

> I guard my gold with thorns for spite,  
> I'm sent for love, I'm laid in white.  
> In gardens grown, on graves I lay —  
> name the bloom that speaks this way.  
> But hold your tongue till the cold's been said —  
> she spoke of frost before the red.  

Both riddles tell them the order. That is deliberate.

## What Vlad says, word for word

### Step 2 — they ask what he waits for

**He needs to hear:** a question word (who / what) **and** a waiting word
(waiting / waits / miss / lost / looking / searching / after). Nearly any
sensible phrasing works — "what are you waiting for", "who do you miss",
"what does he wait for" all trigger.

> Waiting? For six hundred years I have been waiting, and I cannot for the life
> of me remember what for. Her face is gone. Her name is gone. There were two
> words once — the ones she used to say — and I am told they are still written
> somewhere about this place. I cannot go and look. I have no legs. But you do.

### Step 4a — they have only ONE of the two words

**He needs to hear:** one magic word, either offered bare (five words or fewer)
or in a sentence containing a giveaway like *word*, *keyword*, *magic*,
*think*, *guess*.

> One of them. I feel it — something stirs, but I cannot see her yet. There is
> another word. Find it.

### Step 4b — both words, WRONG order ("roses winter")

> Close. Those are the words, but they do not fall in that order. Try them the
> other way about.

### Step 4c — both words, right order ✅

**Accepted:** winter / wintertime, then roses / rose. Any sentence around them
is fine — "I think the words are winter roses" works.

> winter roses... winter roses. Yes. Yes, I can see her now, after all these
> years — thank you for that, truly. Her name was Elena, and she was always,
> always cold. Six centuries ago she told me she had found a great box of hot
> water out in the garden, and that she would warm her bones for just a moment.
> She never came back. If you are braver than I am, go and look.

**"A great box of hot water out in the garden" = the spa pool.**

### Step 6 — they say they found a skull

**He needs to hear:** "skull" plus one of found / find / got / have / seen /
saw / there.

> Elena! After all this time. But look closely at her — something is missing, is
> it not? Tell me what.

### Step 7 — they say the jaw

**He needs to hear:** jaw / jaws / teeth / tooth / mouth, in a **short** phrase
(six words or fewer). A long rambling sentence containing "jaw" is ignored on
purpose, so ordinary chatter cannot give the answer away.

> Her jaw! She cannot speak to me without it. I saw something pale on the table
> in the room where everyone gathers. Bring them both, and find my descendant in
> this house — six hundred years on, one of my family still walks among you
> tonight. Place them in their hands and say the words, or they will not know
> you. Now tell me your name, so that I may curse it kindly.

**"The room where everyone gathers" = the lounge. "My descendant" = Donnie.**

### Step 7b — they give their name

Whatever they say next (within a minute) is taken as their name and written to
the log with a timestamp.

> [Name]. I shall remember it, which is more than I can say for most of the
> living. Now go, and do not forget the words — they will not know you without
> them.

### Any step — "say that again"

Guests can ask Vlad to repeat whatever he last told them. Works for any stage.
Accepted: *say that again*, *what did you say*, *can you repeat that*, *sorry
what was that*, *again*.

> Again? Very well. Listen this time. [the same line, word for word]

⚠️ **Encourage this.** Elena's story is long, the room is loud, and children
will miss half of it. It replays the exact wording rather than a summary, so
nothing gets garbled on the way through.

It expires after a few minutes, so a group arriving cold cannot use it to hear
a stage they have not reached.

## Your job at step 8

⚠️ **Vlad never says Donnie's name.** Working out who is the last part of the
puzzle, and guests are meant to try several adults.

**If a guest brings you the skull and jaw:**

- **and says "winter roses"** → you still do not react. You are not the one.
- **and says nothing** → do not react.

**Stay blank either way.** A raised eyebrow, a laugh, or a glance at Donnie
gives it away and ends the hunt for everyone.

**Only Donnie reacts**, and only to skull **plus** jaw **plus** the words.

## If someone gets stuck

**They cannot find Vlad at all.** Point at nothing. Ask what the card says he
does — "he will answer if you ask aloud" is the hint.

**They are at Vlad but he keeps answering normally.** They have not hit a
trigger. Suggest they ask him a question rather than tell him things. Do not
say the trigger words.

**He answers "one of them" over and over.** They only have one word. Send them
looking again — the other riddle is still hidden.

**He says "they do not fall in that order".** They have both words. That is the
prop working, not failing. They just need to swap them.

**They found the skull but will not go near the spa.** Fine — an adult can
retrieve it. Do not let a child lean over the water.

**Nobody has solved it by late afternoon.** Agree a time with Donnie for a
public nudge — reading the opening card aloud is usually enough.

---

## Things that are NOT faults

- Vlad answering in character when a trigger is missed. He always talks.
- Mishearing in a noisy room. He is told never to admit it, so he will say
  something mysterious instead of "pardon?".
- A pause of a few seconds before he replies. He is thinking.
- The jaw clacking. It is on theme.
